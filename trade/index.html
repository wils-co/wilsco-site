The trading desk. Measured in R.

This is my paper-trading desk. I make the calls by hand on Hyperliquid perps. A harness sizes each trade from a fixed risk budget, enforces hard limits and records the result in R, which is profit or loss per unit of risk. No real money is on the line yet, and I don't publish dollar amounts.

Setups

Nothing counts until thirty trades.

Four trades tell you almost nothing. A 75% win rate over four could easily be luck. So every setup stays marked as incubating until it has at least 30 closed trades and a profit factor above 1 after costs. Only then do I treat it as a real edge.

How it's built

Hard rules, human calls.

Rule 01

The model never places orders

It reads my tickets, lays out the levels and keeps an eye on the rules. A plain Python risk engine has the final say on per-trade risk, the daily loss cap, leverage and drawdown, and I make every call.

Rule 02

The tape is context, not a trigger

A local daemon keeps a running record of Hyperliquid's order book, open interest and liquidation clusters, so I can see where the crowded stops are. It shows me the data. What I do with it is up to me.

Rule 03

Costs come off every trade

Each closed trade has modelled taker fees, slippage and funding taken off before it counts. The fee model is an estimate until I've checked it against real fills, so treat small edges with suspicion.
